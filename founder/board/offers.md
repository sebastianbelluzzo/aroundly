# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Buyers are easy to find and reach.** Venues are physical, local and few enough to visit door to door in two regions. The sales kit, the 22-minute meeting script and the clear tier split (bars, DJ-set venues, clubs) match the offer to the buyer.
- **The pain is real and the pitch names it well.** Organic reach is falling, flyers vanish after 24h and boosts can't be measured. Pitching "acquisition, not broadcasting" puts Aroundly beside Instagram instead of against it.
- **Low effort and quick start for the buyer.** Guided setup, a phone demo, a free first month with no card. The launch price locked for early partners is real urgency, not fake urgency.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood is close to zero today.** The value equation falls apart on proof. The venue is buying reach to an audience that doesn't exist yet: the app is pre-launch, has no users, and its backend was left in test mode and stopped working in summer 2026. "How many users do you have?" is the objection that decides the sale, and right now the answer is nothing. Every feature in the stack (pushes, badges, top-of-home slots) is only worth as much as the audience behind it.
2. **The offer sells features, not an outcome, and the buyer carries all the risk.** The tiers list badges, pushes and dashboards. None of them promises more guests through the door. There is no guarantee, and after the free month comes a 3-paid-month minimum plus 30 days' notice. Asking a bar owner for €150+ a month (plus VAT) with lock-in, for an empty audience, is a high-sacrifice ask. Cutting prices won't fix that. Proof and a guarantee will.
3. **The market's wallet and pain are unproven at these prices.** Bars with music nights often run on thin margins and their current tool, Instagram, is free. We don't know how many venues there are in FVG and Veneto, what they spend on promotion today, or whether the market is growing. Without those numbers the €50 to €250 ladder is a guess. No territorial exclusivity also weakens the reason to buy at the top tier.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many target venues are in FVG and Veneto, and what does each spend on promotion per month today (boosts, PR, promoters, printed flyers)?
- What outcome can you measure and promise a venue: views, saves, shares, tickets sold, people at the door?
- How many active users in one city would make a €50 subscription an easy yes? When will you get there, and how?
- What happens to a venue's money if the audience isn't there in month two?
- What is the ticket commission, and could it replace the subscription at first?
- When will the backend be live and secure again?
- Could you double the Headline price and still be the obvious choice for a club? If not, what is missing?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Build the audience first, in one city.** Show a stated number of active users (founders to set it) before charging any venue.
2. **Take the risk off the venue until there is proof.** For early partners, start free or commission-only, or pair the paid tiers with a measurable guarantee: for example, a refund or free months if a stated threshold of tracked views, saves or ticket sales is missed. Drop the 3-month lock-in until that proof exists.
3. **Rewrite the tiers around outcomes.** Lead with what the venue gets (people reached, tickets sold), with the features listed underneath.
4. **Fix the backend and set the commission** before the next sales visit.
