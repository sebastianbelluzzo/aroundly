# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market you could actually own.** Nightlife in FVG and Veneto is narrow and has edges. If the founders keep it narrower still, one city and one scene, they could get most of the market quickly. That is the right shape for a beachhead.
- **A network effect is possible.** Two sides that feed each other (more venues bring more users, more users bring more venues) is the strongest kind of moat when one city tips. It is also the only durable advantage on offer here.
- **The positioning is sharp.** "Instagram broadcasts, Aroundly acquires" picks out a real gap: people who are actively looking for something tonight and don't know the venue yet.
- **Distribution has been thought through.** A door-to-door kit with a script, a demo and a free first month is a real way to reach venues.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No users, so no product to sell.** It is pre-launch, there is no user plan, and the backend has expired. Venues are being asked to pay for reach that does not exist yet. Without a deliberate way to win users city by city, the network effect works against them: churn after the 3-month minimum, then an empty map.
2. **No breakthrough and no secret.** An event map and calendar is an incremental improvement on Instagram, Google and Telegram groups. Nothing here is ten times better. Nothing stops a local promoter, a ticketing platform or a well-followed Instagram events page from copying it in month six. Selling every competitor in town (no exclusivity) makes it more of a commodity.
3. **Spread too thin for the budget.** Two regions, three tiers, ticketing, pushes, video trailers and dashboards, on €200 to €1,000 a month. Spreading this wide means no single city tips, and density is the whole moat.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about this nightlife scene that the big platforms and local promoters don't?
- Which single city, and which scene within it, will you dominate first? What share of its venues counts as "won"?
- How do the first 1,000 users arrive, at what cost per user, and before or after venues pay?
- How many venues have said yes, even to the free month? How many of those would pay?
- Why now? What changed that makes this possible today and not five years ago?
- What do users get here that a well-run Instagram events page doesn't give them, and is it ten times better?
- Who stops you in year two: a ticketing platform adding a map, or a promoter with 50k followers?
- What does each paying venue cost to win (founder hours, visits) against what it pays over its lifetime?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick one city.** Stop selling region-wide until one city is dense.
2. **Get users before charging venues.** Show a measurable user base in that city (the target number is an open question for the founders to set and justify), e.g. by listing every event for free at first.
3. **Fix the backend first.** Secure the Firebase setup and restore access before any venue goes live.
4. **Name the moat.** Write down in one paragraph which advantage this builds (network density, exclusive data, ticketing lock-in) and how month-six copycats lose.
5. **Get early proof.** Paid conversion and renewal after the 3-month minimum from a first group of venues, with the number to be agreed before the money is released.
