# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The user moment is real and easy to picture: it is 22:00, you want to go out, you open one app and see what is on near you tonight, with time, DJ, genre and price. Today that answer is scattered across Instagram stories, Telegram groups and friends.
- The positioning line ("Instagram reaches your followers; Aroundly reaches people looking for something to do tonight") is clear and does not ask venues to give anything up.
- One region, one category (nightlife only) is a focused place to start.
- The founders already name the core risk (two-sided market) instead of hiding it.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **An empty first five minutes.** The product is the user opening the app tonight and finding it full. With no users, venues will not pay, and with few venues, the map is empty and users delete the app on first launch. Nothing in the plan says how the user side gets a complete calendar before the venues pay. Every other part of the design depends on this.
2. **Selling a product that is not working.** The backend was left in test mode and client access expired in summer 2026. Venues are being asked to commit to 3 paid months for an app that is not public, has no users and has a broken backend. A first demo that fails, or a dashboard that reports nothing, ends the relationship and the referral chain in a small regional scene.
3. **Too much, too early.** Three tiers, badges, pushes, video trailers, PDF reports, ticketing, a partner group and a commission not yet priced: this is a mature ad platform's menu on a product with zero audience. Paid "Trending" and "Recommended" slots also risk making the user feed feel like ads, which damages the trust the user side needs. The pitch to venues needs a paragraph; the pitch to users is one line. Build for the one line.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the one thing Aroundly must do brilliantly? Is it "complete list of tonight's events" or "ad channel for venues"?
- What does a user in Udine or Padova see on the first Saturday night after launch? How many events, and who entered them?
- Could you fill the calendar yourselves (from public flyers and socials) before charging anyone, so the user side works on day one?
- How many active users in an area make a venue's €50 worth it, and how will you show them that number? (Open question: no target exists yet.)
- Who keeps event data accurate when a night is cancelled or the DJ changes? Where does a flyer go from venue to app, and what breaks there?
- When will the backend be fixed, secured and tested, and who owns it among the three founders?
- How do you keep paid placement from making the feed feel like ads to users?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. The backend is fixed, secured (out of test mode) and the app is public before any venue is charged.
2. Cut the offer to one free listing for venues plus one simple paid tier; drop badges, trailers, PDF reports and ticketing until there is an audience to show them to.
3. Seed one city's calendar until it is complete for every weekend for at least a month, then measure real user numbers (weekly active users, saves, shares) and set the threshold at which venues start paying.
4. Mark paid placement clearly as sponsored, and cap it, so the user feed stays useful.
